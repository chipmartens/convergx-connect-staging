Agriculture

Agriculture already solved problems defence has not.

A harvest window with no second chance forced autonomy, cold chain and traceability into production years ago.

The deadline was the weather

The deadline

A season is the unit of time here, and it sets everything else.

The constraint was the season, and a season does not negotiate. Nobody in defence procurement recruits from agriculture, so nobody has looked.

Planting and harvest windows are short. Miss one and the loss is the crop, not the schedule.

So the design requirement became uptime inside the window. A machine that averages well across a year still loses the crop.

Working capital runs on the same clock, because the season sets the cash cycle.

Autonomous field logistics and telematics on equipment working far out of cell range.

ISOBUS interoperability across mixed fleets, and cold chain over roads nobody engineered for it.

Traceability from lot to shelf.

The same problems, filed under a different industry

The same problems

A remote programme has the agriculture problem and none of the agriculture suppliers.

Move materiel over ground with no infrastructure and no operator on board.

Hold a temperature across a long route in bad conditions.

Prove which lot went where, months later, to somebody auditing it.

Written in agricultural language those are autonomy, cold chain and traceability.

The engineering underneath is the same.

The companies that built all of it sell through custom operators, ag retail and a dealer network. No defence sourcing system reads any of those channels, so none of them holds a record of the company.

What that company can also tell you is whether it has ever missed a window.

Nobody in defence procurement has a way to ask.

Food security is now in the national security conversation

The policy ground

Canadian policy discussion in 2026 placed agri-food alongside national security, and dual-use agri-tech alongside the Defence Industrial Strategy.

That is a public policy fact, cited here as context and not as anything ConvergX did.

It puts critical food infrastructure, and the companies that build it, inside programmes they have never bid into.

Being in scope and being findable are not the same thing, and only one of them changed.
