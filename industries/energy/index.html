One qualified vendor owns the schedule.

Long-lead packages are committed during engineering, before construction drawings exist.

If one vendor clears qualification, the schedule and the sanction economics both belong to that vendor.

There is a second source for that package.

It has never been on your approved vendor list, because your approved vendor list is a record of who supplied the last project.

The list is closed by construction

The closed list

Vendor prequalification ranks the companies already on the list. A company that has never supplied a capital project never reaches the ranking.

An owner's engineer writes the scope of supply from what has been supplied before. The list grows one project at a time, from the same set.

So a single-source package stays single-source through FEED and into expediting. By then a slip is no longer a sourcing decision.

It is capital cost variance against the sanction estimate, and it is reported under somebody's name.

New work sharpens it. A grid interconnection, or an electrification package on a brownfield site, has no last project to build the list from.

Prequalification tells you a vendor is capable.

Nothing in it tells you what else that vendor has committed to for the same delivery window.

What ConvergX does not do here

The boundary

ConvergX does not prequalify vendors. Technical qualification and HSE prequalification are separate gates, run by separate people at your organisation, and both still run.

ConvergX finds the company, asks who it is and what it has actually delivered, and makes the introduction itself.

Your gates then open exactly as they do now, on a company that has already been checked once.
