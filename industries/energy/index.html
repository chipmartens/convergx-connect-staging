One qualified vendor owns the schedule.

Long-lead packages are committed during engineering, before construction drawings exist. If one vendor clears qualification, the schedule and the sanction economics both belong to that vendor.

There is a second source for that package. It has never been on your approved vendor list, because your approved vendor list is a record of who supplied the last project.

The list is closed by construction

Industries / Energy

Vendor prequalification ranks the companies already on the list. A company that has never supplied a capital project never reaches the ranking.

An owner's engineer writes the scope of supply from what has been supplied before. The list grows one project at a time, from the same set.

So a single-source package stays single-source through FEED and into expediting. By then a slip is no longer a sourcing decision. It is a variance you report.

New work sharpens it. A grid interconnection, or an electrification package on a brownfield site, has no last project to build the list from.

What ConvergX does not do here

Boundary

ConvergX does not prequalify vendors. Technical qualification and HSE prequalification are separate gates, run by separate people at your organisation, and both still run.

ConvergX finds the company, checks who it is and what it has actually delivered, and brokers the introduction. Your gates open after that, on a candidate somebody already stood behind.
