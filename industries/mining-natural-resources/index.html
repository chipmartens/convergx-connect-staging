Qualify the second supplier before the first fails.

A safety-critical component lets go on a fly-in site. The only company that makes it is on another continent.

Emergency work costs more than the same work planned, and freight into a remote site costs more and takes longer than freight anywhere else.

Availability, cost per tonne and unplanned downtime hours absorb all of it.

Critical spares and min-max cover the part you predicted. Neither covers a single qualified source going quiet on a safety-critical item.

Finding an alternate source is not qualifying one

Qualifying, not finding

There is usually a shop that can make the part.

Whether it can make it to the drawing, on a safety-critical item, is a different question answered by different people.

An OEM order is a purchase decision. An aftermarket source on a safety-critical component is an engineering one, and it has to be settled before the failure rather than during it.

A maintenance and repair contract covers the equipment it was written against. Qualifying a source outside it belongs to nobody's week until the unit is down.

Whether a shop can hold a safety-critical scope at that distance is a judgement about the shop.

It is not a document it can send you.

Participation commitments get audited

The commitment

In a modern treaty area, participation obligations are legally enforceable and administered by named bodies.

Procurement documents screen out contractors with no credible participation strategy well before technical evaluation.

Those obligations are set and enforced by others. ConvergX had no hand in writing them and claims none.

ConvergX does not certify Indigenous business status, does not administer a participation plan, and does not audit anyone's committed spend.

ConvergX does the part that stalls: finding an Indigenous business or a local contractor that can hold the scope, and checking that it can before the commitment comes up for report.

Certification status is not part of that check. A company states its certifications and ConvergX records them as stated.

Confirming one against the issuing body is your step, and it stays your step.

The second source and the commitment are one job

One job

A second source on a safety-critical item and a participation commitment you can demonstrate are the same job: qualifying a company nobody on site has worked with.

Your site list is built from whoever supplied the last shutdown. A company that has never been on it leaves nothing behind for your systems to read.

ConvergX works it by hand, one requirement at a time.

Who the company is, what it has actually delivered, and whether it is in a position to deliver it again for you.

A site list records who has already been on site. That is the whole of what it records.

If you can hold a safety-critical scope at distance, ConvergX asks you about that claim itself before it puts anyone in front of you.
