Your name is not on the requirement you post

Nobody is measured on the gaps they surfaced.

The board carries no author to another attendee on any post, whether or not it was flagged anonymous. Your identity moves once, when you agree to an introduction.

Naming the gap costs you the disclosure

A capability gap is a statement you make in front of the people who sell into it.

Raise it in a hallway and you have told whoever was standing there.

Put it in a public solicitation and you have disclosed it to the market, on a procurement clock, with the programme attached.

Either route costs you the disclosure up front, before it has produced anything at all, and a disclosure cannot be withdrawn once it is made.

What everyone else can see

There are three states. What reaches ConvergX does not change, and everyone else on the board reads a requirement with nobody attached to it.

Confidentiality ends at the introduction

When you agree to meet, the other company learns who you are.

ConvergX does not broker an anonymous introduction, because an introduction nobody can name is answerable to nobody.

Where the situation needs one, an NDA covers the exchange before the introduction rather than after it.

An anonymous post can reach a company nobody in the room could have named.

Posting a requirement is not disclosing it

What you write is a sentence about a gap, with nobody's name on it, read by one organisation.
