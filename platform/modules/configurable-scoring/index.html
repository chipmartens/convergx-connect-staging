Configurable Scoring

Set what counts as a good fit, sector by sector

What matters in aerospace is not what matters in agriculture, and one fixed formula quietly pretends otherwise. A ConvergX administrator sets what the scoring weighs for each sector, so a result can be explained by pointing at what produced it.

Request access

01

Weight the scoring for the work in front of you

A ConvergX administrator sets what counts and by how much, for each sector and each kind of work. A first-article requirement and a long-lead fabrication do not deserve the same weighting.

  • Per sector and per kind of work
  • Set by ConvergX, not either side

02

Explain a score by pointing at what made it

The weights are visible to the person who set them, so a scoring result can be explained rather than asserted. That is the difference between a score somebody can defend and a verdict.

  • Weights visible to whoever set them
  • A score, not a verdict

03

Change the weighting and change what surfaces

Adjusting what is weighed changes which companies reach the shortlist, which is the point of it being adjustable at all. Buyers and suppliers neither set these weights nor get to read them.

  • Weighting drives the shortlist
  • Neither side sets or reads it

Common questions

Configurable Scoring
Who sets the weights?

A ConvergX administrator, per sector and per kind of work. Buyers and suppliers do not set them and cannot see them.

Can I see why a company scored the way it did?

The weights are visible to the person who set them, so a result can be explained by pointing at what produced it.

Is this the match standard?

No. What makes a good match sits in a person's head and has never been written down. Weighting is a different thing, and writing the standard down is an open design problem.
