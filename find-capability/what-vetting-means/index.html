A person at ConvergX looks at the company on the other side and decides whether you two belong in a room. That decision has an owner, and it is not you.

Every other route hands you a name nobody signed for

01 / The signature

The recommendation arrives unattached. Whoever produced it is not in the conversation on the day the capability turns out to be smaller than the claim.

Nothing that was ranked, compiled or delivered as a document carries a continuing obligation. Nobody put anything at stake in producing it.

ConvergX puts its name on the introduction. It stays attached to that decision, and it can be asked about it a year later.

What the name on it buys you

02 / Accountability

The company on the other side was chosen, not returned. If it cannot do what it said it could, ConvergX is the party that chose it, and ConvergX is still there.

You begin qualification with a candidate an organisation already put its standing behind. That does not shorten the process. It changes what you spend the process looking for.

ConvergX composes the same room next year, and the year after. A bad introduction sits in that room with it.

The accountable party is an organisation

03 / Structure

An institution can be held to a decision for as long as it exists.

You already price this. Every sole source you carry is a judgement about whether an organisation will still deliver, not about the individual who signs for it. This is the same judgement, pointed at ConvergX.

Which means nobody's departure changes what ConvergX is answerable for.

This ran by hand before it ran on a platform

04 / Proof

ConvergX matched primes to suppliers by hand for years before any software existed. The platform records the decision. It does not make it.
