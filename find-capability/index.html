The job nobody owns

Demand side

A requirement your own supply base cannot fill sits with nobody. Sourcing works from a list. Nobody's annual review has a line for finding a company that has never worked for you.

So it goes quietly back to the incumbent, and shows up later as a schedule variance somebody has to report. Finding an alternative was never the hard part. Knowing whether it would hold was.

Where an unfillable requirement usually goes
RouteWhat it can answer, and what it cannot
A supplier database A database can only rank the companies already inside it. A company with no record has nothing for it to rank.
A government registry A registry returns keyword matches against titles written in government language. The company that can help you described itself for a different industry.
A consulting engagement A consulting engagement hands over a deck and leaves. Nobody stays attached to the names in it.
The requirement itself Naming the gap in a room full of vendors is a disclosure. So it does not get named, and it does not get worked.
The people you already call The one route that already answers the right question, and the most reliable thing you have. It reaches as far as the industries you have bought from, and no further.

At their best the first three return a company that looks right on paper. None of them tells you whether it can take your work this quarter. The fourth is why the requirement never reaches any of them. The fifth answers the question you actually have, and it stops at the edge of the industries you already buy from.
