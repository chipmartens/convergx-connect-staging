An organisation selling into one industry could not hold the other three

The position

Aerospace and defence, agriculture, construction, energy, manufacturing, military, mining and natural resources, technology. ConvergX convenes them together. The Congress has run for ten-plus years.

It sits in each of them as the organiser, never as a participant.

That is the only position from which one industry can be asked about another, and the only one with nothing riding on the answer.

The four are not adjacent markets. They qualify differently, buy on different cycles and answer to different regulators, and ConvergX has to be credible in each on its own terms.

The Congress is one of three things

The organisation

ConvergX runs an international Congress, regional events with a host region, and a consulting practice.

By ConvergX's own account, the shortage was never solutions. It was connection between the industries that already had them.

What began as one forum is now three, and the premise has not changed.

The review asks two sets of questions.

How we vet

By whom: ConvergX, the organisation, with its standing attached to the outcome.

Against what: two sets of questions. One a file can answer. One it cannot.

Vetted, not scraped. Nothing here is assembled from public records and ranked. Someone at ConvergX reads it, asks, and decides.

Both sets are questions. ConvergX publishes no rating and issues no score. If reliability could be scored, the records would already carry it.

What the file answers

The floor

These four are answered before a company is put in front of anyone. An unanswered one stops the introduction.

Is the company real. The legal entity, who owns it, and where it operates.

Is the capability deployed, or described. Where it runs today, who it runs for, and at what scale.

Is the work commercialised, at TRL 8 to 9. Research and early-stage go to a separate ConvergX arm.

Should this introduction happen at all. Ownership, control and influence, and any conflict sitting on either side of it.

They are also the part any competent process would run, and ConvergX claims nothing unusual for them.

What the file cannot answer

The questions

Four more, and none of them has a document behind it. ConvergX asks. What comes back is judgement, and it is recorded as judgement.

Can they take it on now. What else the company is carrying, and what it says when asked directly.

Who actually did the work. Whether the people behind the reference are still there.

What happened the last time something went wrong. Asked of the company directly, and recorded as the company answered it.

Would ConvergX put its own name on this one. The question the other three are for.

Qualified is on paper. Reliable is not.

The review answers one question

Scope

Should these two meet.

It is not a supplier qualification and does not stand in for one. First-article inspection, special-process approval and export classification are your gates, run by your people, on your programme.

What changes is who you are running them on. The wrong company is cheapest to find out about now, and most expensive to find out about at first article.

The standard does not leave when a person does

Durability

A contact list leaves with the person holding it.

The vetting standard belongs to ConvergX, is applied by ConvergX, and is recorded by ConvergX. People move. What the review has to answer does not.

The answers are a different matter. What a review turns up about a company sits with the people who asked, and moving more of that into the organisation is what ConvergX is building.

ConvergX puts its name on the introduction.

The name belongs to the organisation, so it is still there to be asked about the decision after the people who made it have gone.

That is the part you need years after the introduction.

What a name here does not carry

Names change. What a role is answerable for does not.

No biographies. No tenure. No advisory positions, no board seats, no career history. ConvergX publishes those on its own site and they are not what this page is for.

A role is answerable to ConvergX for the decision it carries. The standard it applies, and the record of what it decided, belong to ConvergX and not to the person holding it.

Who we convene

Nothing about this room accumulated. No list grew, no directory filled up. Both sides were composed, company by company.

A room that fills itself holds whoever turned up, and it can only answer with what those companies already sell.

ConvergX composes across industries rather than inside one.

Composing inside one would have been faster, and it would have produced a room where everyone already knows everyone.

Composition is the work

Composition

Every company on either side was weighed against the room before it was in it.

ConvergX decides in both directions. Whether a capability is worth putting in front of the people who carry requirements, and whether a requirement is worth putting in front of the companies that could answer it.

Composing is slower than admitting, and it is the only way to end up having asked a company the things a list cannot answer. The difference shows up when a requirement goes in.

Neither decision runs automatically, and neither one is delegated. How we vet

Who is deliberately not here

The exclusions

A room anyone can enter tells you nothing about the company across the table.

Companies still proving a technology. ConvergX runs a separate arm for early-stage and research work, and that is where it goes. Different job, different room.

Anyone who has not been through the review. That is the only way in, and it runs in both directions.

The exclusions are what make an invitation mean anything.
