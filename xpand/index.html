The last mile

By the time a solution reaches a new industry, the engineering is usually the settled part.

ConvergX names three things that stop one anyway.

Procurement pathways. A buyer who wants the thing still has to find a route to buy it, and in a regulated sector that route is written down, long, and older than the problem.

Compliance and supplier qualification. The standard a new industry holds its suppliers to is rarely the one the solution was built against, and qualifying is its own project.

Financing. Somebody carries the cost between the decision and the deployment, and it is not always obvious who.

Commercialization is ConvergX's word for closing those three. It is the distance between a solution that works and a solution somebody has bought.

The scale ConvergX states for Xpand starts at TRL 7.

What readiness means

Technology Readiness Level 7 and above. ConvergX publishes that as the scope of an Xpand engagement.

TRL is NASA's scale, one to nine, and the question it asks is whether the thing works.

ConvergX states that its own emphasis is a different question, which is whether the thing will be adopted and will scale. It names the Commercial Readiness Level scale, one to nine, for that one, and it acknowledges Manufacturing Readiness Level as the framework some industries use instead.
